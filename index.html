Project 01 — Data Engineering

A monthly analytics-ready dataset for Bangladesh's largest telecom

Grameenphone was selected for its long, consistently audited annual reports and a calendar-year fiscal cycle. I combined daily stock prices (resampled to month-end), annual firm fundamentals (revenue, total assets, EPS from audited annual reports 2020–2024), and Bangladesh macroeconomic indicators (GDP growth, CPI inflation from the World Bank API) into one clean, merge-ready monthly panel.

Project 02 — Volatility & Risk Modeling

Quantifying Target's tail risk with GARCH(1,1)

Moving from levels to risk, I modeled TGT's daily log-return volatility through two pandemic-and-inflation market regimes. An ADF test confirmed the return series is stationary, but the distribution is sharply non-normal — excess kurtosis of 36.5 and skew of −2.19 — driven by isolated shocks like the May 2022 single-day earnings gap of roughly −25%. That fat-tailed behavior ruled out a standard normal error term in favor of a Student's-t specification.

Project 03 — Return Forecasting

ARIMA vs. LSTM: does deep learning earn its complexity?

Building on Projects 1–2, I forecast TGT's daily log returns with two competing approaches: a classical ARIMA(1,0,3) benchmark selected by AIC grid search, and a single-layer LSTM (10-day sliding window → 50 units → dense output) trained with leakage-safe, train-only scaling. Both were scored once on an identical, untouched 190-day test window — never used for tuning.

Decision — Per the project's decision rule, test RMSE is the primary criterion, MAE a stability check, and directional accuracy a secondary signal. LSTM's RMSE edge was roughly 1.2% — within noise — while its directional accuracy was actually worse than ARIMA's. Daily equity returns are close to white noise, so the added complexity of an LSTM did not earn its keep here. ARIMA(1,0,3) was selected as the simpler, faster, more auditable model. Re-fit on the full sample, it projects a mildly positive cumulative log return of approximately +0.41% over the next 30 trading days — a low-confidence point estimate, not a trading signal.

Responsible AI Use

AI disclosure

AI tools were used as a productivity layer across all three projects — never as a substitute for sourcing, judgment, or final review.

Tool used: Claude (Anthropic)

How AI was and wasn't used

Across all three projects, Claude was used as a coding and writing assistant — drafting and debugging Python, structuring documentation, and helping draft narrative text for memos. Every project folder includes a prompt log recording what was asked, what AI produced, and how it was verified.

What AI did not do: it did not retrieve, invent, or estimate any stock price, financial statement figure, or macroeconomic indicator — every data point traces to a cited source (investing.com, audited annual reports, the World Bank API, or Yahoo Finance via yfinance). It did not make the methodological calls — like leaving April 2020 blank instead of forward-filling it, choosing the ARIMA order via AIC grid search, or applying train-only scaling to prevent LSTM leakage. Those were my decisions, which AI helped implement once made.

Verification: for each project I ran the full pipeline myself, inspected automated quality checks (15/15 PASS on Project 1), confirmed the ARIMA/GARCH/LSTM specifications and out-of-sample metrics independently, and reviewed every number in the memos before submission. I take full responsibility for the accuracy, sourcing, and integrity of all work in this portfolio.
